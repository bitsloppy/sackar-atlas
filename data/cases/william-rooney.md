## Who was Bill Rooney

William "Bill" Antony Rooney was 35 years old when he died on 20 February 1986. He lived in Gwynneville — a suburb of Wollongong — with his de facto partner Wayne Davis.

Bill was an openly gay man. He had a reputation among friends as someone who could drink a fair bit and still remain on his feet. That detail — that he could "hold his liquor well" — would later become significant when police moved to explain his catastrophic head injuries as an accidental fall while drunk.

## Valentine's Day, 1986

On the evening of Thursday 13 February 1986, Bill and Wayne drove into Wollongong and spent several hours at the Tattersalls Hotel, drinking schooners. At around 9:00–10:00pm, Wayne headed to a friend's place in Keiraville. Bill, in extremely good spirits and not overly affected by drink, told Wayne he planned to go to Annabel's Disco in Crown Lane.

His movements between then and the following morning are not fully known. A friend, Steven Snedden, saw him at the Grand Hotel in Keira Street that night — describing him as "pretty full ... still walking around but full." He may also have visited Annabel's Disco after midnight.

At 8:40am on Friday 14 February — Valentine's Day — the manager of L&B Discounts walked to the rear of his store and found a man lying between the toilet block and a concrete retaining wall in Crown Lane, near Annabel's Disco. That man was Bill Rooney.

He was alive but semi-conscious, suffering from catastrophic head injuries. His pants and underwear were lowered. He was missing one shoe and one sock. He had no identification — only a Medicare card and $5 in cash. A concrete rock was visible near where he lay. Video footage taken by a WIN Television crew at the scene later showed Bill attempting to pull up his pants by lifting his hips.

Bill was transported to Wollongong Hospital. He died six days later, on 20 February 1986, without regaining full consciousness. The cause of death was massive subdural cerebral haemorrhage and basal skull fractures — blunt head injuries.

## Persons of interest

Two men became persons of interest in Bill's death.

**Leslie John Harrison** (known as "Radar") was briefly investigated in the days after Bill was found. Wayne Davis told police that Bill had previously spoken of a man by that name who was a "well-known poofter basher." Harrison was brought to Wollongong Police Station on 17 February and questioned about his movements. He claimed an alibi — that he had spent the night with his girlfriend. Police made one attempt to locate her, without success. They never interviewed her. Harrison was not the subject of any further enquiry after 17 February 1986. He died in 2004.

Sackar J found the failure to verify Harrison's alibi "not only unsatisfactory but inexplicable."

**Mark Anthony Scerri** emerged as a key person of interest around 1989. Between March 1986 and September 1989 — beginning just three weeks after Bill was found in Crown Lane — twelve men were violently assaulted and sexually assaulted in and around Wollongong. Many were gay. Scerri was arrested in September 1989 and ultimately convicted in November 1992 on seven sexual assault charges relating to three of the victims. His method: strike the victim over the head with a rock, then sexually assault them.

One attack occurred on the other side of the same laneway — Crown Lane — where Bill Rooney had been found in February 1986. Another victim, in relation to whom Scerri was acquitted, claimed his attacker held a rock above his head during the assault and said: *"I'll kill you like I killed the poofter in the laneway."*

Between 1991 and 1993, Detective Inspector Ainsworth — who had been assigned to the investigation of most of the twelve attacks — referred the Rooney case twice to the ODPP for consideration of murder charges against Scerri. Both times, the ODPP declined. Without evidence that Bill had been sexually assaulted, the prosecution could not rely on similar fact evidence from Scerri's other attacks. That evidence never existed because no sexual assault examination had been performed on Bill — either in hospital or at post-mortem.

In May 2023, Scerri was questioned in a private hearing of the Inquiry. That evidence remains confidential.

## The police investigation

The officer in charge at Wollongong Police Station was Constable Michael Tranby.

The investigation was fatally compromised from the start.

**The scene was never secured.** Before the Scientific Investigation Section arrived, a shopkeeper had already hosed down the area where Bill was found. Forensic evidence — whatever the laneway might have held — was gone.

**No exhibits were collected or retained.** There is no record of any exhibit being taken from the scene or from Bill's clothing.

**No sexual assault examination was conducted.** Despite the circumstances in which Bill was found — pants lowered, missing a shoe and sock, possible fingernail marks on his neck — nothing was communicated to his treating doctors or to the forensic pathologist about those circumstances. No anogenital examination was performed either in hospital or during the post-mortem. Sackar J found the NSWPF must accept at least partial responsibility for this failure.

The consequences of that single omission reverberated for decades. When Detective Inspector Ainsworth referred the case to the ODPP in 1993, Mark Tedeschi QC concluded there was insufficient evidence to charge Scerri — *specifically because* no sexual assault examination had been conducted, meaning similar fact evidence from Scerri's other attacks could not be deployed.

On 14 February 1986, the day Bill was found, police recorded "suspicious circumstances." On 20 February, when Bill died, Constable Tranby's Report of Death to Coroner noted "SUSPICIOUS CIRCUMSTANCES ARE RELATED TO THIS DECEASED." By January 1987, Detective Senior Constable Tate's formal statement expressed the opinion that Bill had fallen while intoxicated. The documented basis for this shift — from suspicious to accidental — does not exist.

When Wayne Davis attended Wollongong Police Station on 14 February and explained that he was Bill's de facto partner, the dynamic in the room changed. Subsequent investigation followed the assumption of an accidental fall.

The NSWPF conceded in submissions that the investigation was inadequate in several respects: the scene should have been secured; exhibits should have been collected; and there was no reason consistent with proper police practice why a sexual assault examination was not requested.

## Strike Force Parrabell

Strike Force Parrabell reviewed the Rooney case as part of its paper review of cold cases. Its Bias Crime Indicators Form (BCIF) answered all ten indicators as "No Evidence of Bias Crime." Its overall categorisation was "Insufficient Information."

These two findings cannot be reconciled. The BCIF itself explicitly acknowledged: the presence of a concrete rock at the scene consistent with Scerri's known method; Scerri's convictions for attacks on gay men in the same location beginning three weeks after Bill was found; and that "it is likely the original Coronial finding regarding Mr Rooney's death being caused by a fall is incorrect." Yet every single indicator was answered: No Evidence of Bias Crime.

Sackar J found this "indicates some considerable confusion and imprecision in the methodology of Strike Force Parrabell" — consistent with his broader findings about Parrabell's approach across all cases it reviewed.

For some time, Parrabell had been unable to locate any material on the case at all because it had incorrectly recorded Rooney's surname as "Rudney."

## What Sackar found

*"I find that Mr Rooney died on 20 February 1986 as a result of blunt head injuries sustained on 14 February 1986. However, the evidence available to the Inquiry is insufficient to establish whether these injuries were the result of an assault or an accidental fall. I note that this finding is consistent with the previous coronial findings."*

*"In my view, on the evidence available to the Inquiry, there is objectively reason to suspect that LGBTIQ bias was a factor in the death of Mr Rooney."*

The medical evidence — reviewed by independent forensic pathologist Dr Linda Iles — could not exclude either an accidental fall or an assault with a blunt instrument. Sackar J agreed the manner of death could not be positively established. But he found the combination of factors — Bill's lowered pants and missing shoe, the concrete rock at the scene, Scerri's nearby attacks on gay men beginning three weeks later, and the alleged admission made during the Crown Lane attack in 1989 — provided objectively sufficient grounds to suspect LGBTIQ bias was a factor.

Sackar J proposed no recommendations arising from the Inquiry's consideration of Bill Rooney's death.

No one has ever been charged with Bill Rooney's death. His remains were cremated. His de facto partner Wayne Davis — who spent years carrying these events — is also deceased.
