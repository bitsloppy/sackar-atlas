## Who was Crispin Dye

Crispin Wilson Dye was 41 years old when he was killed in Darlinghurst on Christmas morning 1993. A music publisher and musician, he had lived mostly in Cairns, Queensland, and was visiting Sydney at the time of his death. Contemporary press described him as having managed AC/DC, one of Australia's most prominent rock bands.

Dye was drinking in and around Oxford Street, Surry Hills — a known gay and lesbian social precinct — on the night he was attacked. He was last seen heavily intoxicated in Oxford Street at around 4:00am.

Dye's immediate family are all deceased. His parents Ian and Jean Dye predeceased him; his brother Brenton Dye died in 2018. In 2023, the Inquiry briefed his cousin Lisa Colnan on the work it had done in relation to Crispin's death.

## The night of 22 December 1993

At around 4:30am on 23 December 1993, Crispin Dye was found lying face down on the road in the laneway behind Kinselas nightclub — Little Oxford Street, at the intersection with Campbell Street, Darlinghurst. About five minutes later, three men were seen standing around him, "picking him up" and "moving him around." Police and paramedics attended. Officers found coins scattered in the area where Dye lay, and his wallet was subsequently discovered to be missing.

When found, Dye was alive but in cardiac arrest. He was transported to St Vincent's Hospital at 5:07am and then transferred to St George Hospital, Kogarah, at around 1:30pm on 23 December 1993. He died from his injuries on Christmas Day, 25 December 1993.

Post-mortem examination by Dr Liliana Schwartz established that Dye died from a head injury. He had sustained at least three blows to the face and left side of his head from a blunt instrument — possibly a baton or the edge of a cricket bat. He had also sustained defensive wounds to his wrist. His blood alcohol level at admission had been 0.260g per 100mL.

## Strike Force Barcoo and the original investigation

Strike Force Barcoo was established on 29 December 1993 to investigate Dye's death. Led by Detective Sergeant Geoffrey Roy Knight, the investigation gathered considerable information and pursued various persons of interest — including NP128 (adverse findings on evidence and alibi, but insufficient evidence to charge) and Richard Leonard (who made admissions to killing Dye but was later excluded). No charges were ever laid.

Strike Force Barcoo was deactivated after the August 1995 inquest. Reactivated in January 1999 following further information, it was ultimately deactivated again without charge.

In 2023, clothes and other exhibits held by police from the original investigation were sent to FASS for forensic re-testing — the first time this had ever been done. The testing was significant: FASS found two pieces of paper that had been folded in the pocket of Dye's shirt for thirty years without anyone knowing they were there. Police conceded at hearing that this was "a significant oversight."

## Strike Force Parrabell — the binary approach

A Bias Crimes Indicators Form (BCIF) was completed by Strike Force Parrabell. The overall categorisation was "Insufficient Information," driven by what Sackar found to be an impermissible binary approach: the assumption that where robbery is apparent, bias is negated.

In a January 2017 dip-sample review, BCU Sergeant Steer attempted to explain to Strike Force Parrabell that "motivation need only be partially bias" and that targeting a group because of perceived vulnerability "can be bias motivation." The Strike Force officers resisted this view and maintained their "Insufficient Information" finding.

Sackar found the NSWPF's defence of this approach to be "unconvincing," noting that the stance taken by Strike Force Parrabell was one of many examples of the NSWPF's "overly defensive approach" to the Inquiry.

## The 2023 DNA discovery

In 2023, at the direction of the Sackar Inquiry, FASS conducted forensic re-testing on exhibits retained from 1993. Two highly significant discoveries were made:

1. **Two pieces of paper** — a yellow post-it note with a handwritten name ("Garry Hook") and phone number, and a thick white card with "Davidoff Cool Water" written on it — were found folded in the pocket of Dye's shirt. These items had not been noticed or tested in thirty years.

2. **A DNA profile** — designated "Unknown Male B" — was extracted from a hair found on Dye's shirt. This profile has not been matched to any person on DNA databases.

Sackar made Recommendation 4 in relation to this case: that the NSWPF regularly monitor DNA databases for the "Unknown Male B" profile, and that the white card be kept securely in the event that future forensic technology can determine whether a brown mark on it is a fingerprint.

## What Sackar found

Sackar upheld the coronial finding of State Coroner Hand:

> On 25 December 1993 at the St George Hospital, Kogarah, Crispin Wilson Dye died of the effects of a head injury inflicted on 23 December 1993 in Campbell Street, Darlinghurst, by a person or persons unknown.

He found that, on the available evidence, there is "objectively reason to suspect that LGBTIQ bias was a factor in Mr Dye's death." He rejected the binary robbery-or-bias approach taken by Strike Force Parrabell, finding that the possibility of a robbery motive did not exclude the possibility of LGBTIQ bias. No person has ever been charged with any offence in relation to Crispin Dye's death.
