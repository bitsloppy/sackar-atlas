## Who was Raymond Keam

Raymond Frederick Keam was 43 years old when he was killed in Alison Park, Randwick, in the early hours of 13 January 1987. He was a married man and father; his son's name was Dane.

Little biographical detail was recorded by the Sackar Inquiry, which deliberately did not investigate Keam's death because criminal proceedings were already underway at the time the Inquiry was conducting its work (SCOI Vol 2, para 5.29).

## Alison Park — a known gay beat

Alison Park, Randwick, was a known gay beat. Raymond Keam's body was found face up in the grass in the early morning of 13 January 1987, with various injuries. A post-mortem examination later established he had died from a brain haemorrhage.

Stanley Bruce Early — who at the time of the murder went by the name Stanley "Spider" Sutton — lived in a boarding house directly opposite the park. Evidence at trial established that Early had on occasion been part of a group who "set upon" men in the park late at night because they believed them to be gay.

Crown Prosecutor Ken McKay SC argued at trial that Early had targeted men he perceived to be gay — including making homophobic remarks to neighbours, bragging about attacking a car he said belonged to "a poofter", and boasting to others about violence at the park. A neighbour told the court that Early and a group of young men had nearly attacked him while he was walking through the park at night.

Police alleged that the attack on Keam was motivated by a false perception of his sexual orientation. Raymond Keam was not necessarily gay; the evidence establishes that he was perceived as gay by his attackers.

## The 1987 investigation

The original Randwick police investigation did not identify a perpetrator. The case remained unsolved for more than three decades.

## Strike Force Parrabell and the lead to Stanley Early

Strike Force Parrabell (2015–2018), which reviewed 88 suspected gay hate homicide cases, included Keam's death as Case 22. While the Inquiry identified Keam as a Category A case (confirmed LGBTIQ hate crime), the SCOI itself did not individually investigate his death because criminal proceedings were already underway when the Inquiry commenced (SCOI Vol 2, para 5.29).

Parrabell's community outreach component generated publicity that yielded additional lines of inquiry. These led to the identification of Stanley Early and his arrest in August 2021.

## The conviction of Stanley Early

Stanley Bruce Early was convicted of murder on 7 June 2023 in the NSW Supreme Court, after a jury trial.

On 20 October 2023, Justice Dina Yehia sentenced Early — then aged 77 — to a maximum of 22 years in prison.

The conviction of Stanley Early is one of only two successful prosecutions arising from the 88 cases examined by Strike Force Parrabell (the other being the manslaughter conviction of Scott White for the death of Scott Johnson in 2023).
