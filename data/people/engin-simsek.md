Engin Simsek was identified in May 2023 by the Sackar Inquiry as a person likely involved in the death of Ernest Head in 1976. A palm print in blood found on the kitchen wall above Head's body was matched to Simsek using V11 NAFIS technology — a match that had not been possible in earlier reviews (2002, 2004, 2005) due to the limitations of earlier fingerprint matching algorithms.

Simsek was born in Türkiye on 3 September 1943 and emigrated to Australia on 31 May 1972. His intended address on arrival was on Grosvenor Crescent — the same street where Ernest Head lived and died. His record fingerprints had been in police files since his arrest in 1980 for malicious injury.

In August 1994, Simsek departed Australia. He died by suicide in Türkiye on 6 May 1999. No criminal proceedings are now possible.

The Inquiry could not make a positive finding as to the precise role Simsek played in Head's death. His sexuality is unknown, and his motive cannot be determined with confidence. Sackar found there is "objectively reason to suspect that LGBTIQ bias was a factor" in Head's death, noting the temporal proximity between sexual activity and the frenzied nature of the attack.
