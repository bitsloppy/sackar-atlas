Mark Anthony Scerri was identified as the key person of interest in the death of Bill Rooney. He was never charged with Rooney's death.

Between March 1986 and September 1989 — beginning just three weeks after Bill Rooney was found in Crown Lane — Scerri attacked twelve men in and around Wollongong. Nine identified him as the attacker. His method was consistent across attacks: strike the victim over the head (typically with a rock or brick), then sexually assault them.

He was arrested on 26 September 1989 and charged with 29 offences relating to twelve men. In November 1992, he was convicted on seven sexual assault charges relating to three men, all attacked in 1989. One attack occurred on the other side of Crown Lane from where Rooney was found. On 19 February 1993, he was sentenced to 16 years' imprisonment, with a non-parole period of 12 years. He was released on parole on 24 November 2001.

Less than six months after release, on 12 May 2002, Scerri attacked a 26-year-old man in circumstances similar to his earlier offending. He was arrested on 16 October 2002, pleaded guilty in February 2003 to sexual assault, and on 12 September 2003 was sentenced to an effective term of eight years with a six-year non-parole period.

One of the acquitted 1989 victims (referred to as I186 in SCOI proceedings) claimed that during his own attack — in December 1986, ten months after Rooney's death — his assailant held a rock above his head and said: *"I'll kill you like I killed the poofter in the laneway."*

Detective Inspector Ainsworth referred the Rooney case to the ODPP twice (1991 and 1993) for consideration of murder charges against Scerri. Both times the ODPP declined — primarily because no sexual assault examination had been conducted on Rooney, foreclosing reliance on similar fact evidence.

In May 2023, Scerri was questioned in a private hearing of the Sackar Inquiry. That evidence remains confidential.

The case summary prepared by Strike Force Parrabell stated: "it is likely the original Coronial finding regarding Mr Rooney's death being caused by a fall is incorrect" — and identified Scerri as the probable perpetrator — yet simultaneously answered all ten Bias Crime Indicators as "No Evidence of Bias Crime."
